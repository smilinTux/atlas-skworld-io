Meet Atlas, your operations agent

Every fleet needs an operator: someone who watches the services, notices when something breaks, and fixes it. In SKWorld, that someone is an AI. Atlas holds the operations chair across every app, and the human keeps exactly one card: the freeze.

How it works

One loop, every fifteen minutes, plus an immediate wake on any alert. It checks the freeze first, observes every app, reasons about what is firing, decides, and acts or escalates.

wake -> freeze check -> observe every app -> reason -> classify each fix -> auto-apply (signed) or escalate to you -> report + audit

It acts through three channels. Ops fixes are signed object changes. Code fixes go through the autocode test gate. Architecture changes come to you as two or three options, then flow to code.

§The Constitution

The small set of rules that make the autonomy safe. Every one is enforced in code, covered by tests, and proven on real builds. Atlas cannot rewrite any of them.

  1. Freeze always wins. A human-owned kill switch halts all actuation instantly. Atlas can never touch it.
  2. Irreversible actions escalate. Delete, drain an always-on node, fleet restart: never automatic. You choose from the options.
  3. The constitutional carve-out. Atlas can rewrite almost anything, but a change to its own guardrails can never auto-merge, even at a perfect score with green CI. It cannot loosen its own leash.
  4. Every write is signed. Atlas signs under its own identity, so the audit trail always shows who did what.
  5. Every action is ITIL-governed. Standard fixes auto-approve, majors need your call, and Atlas can never self-authorize a major change.
